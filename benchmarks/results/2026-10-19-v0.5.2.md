## Conditions

- Ethernet connected, 1920x1080, screen timeout 300 s, animation scales 1.0. The IP is omitted
  from every published file.
- 149 packages, 23 disabled, 11 third-party. No package was disabled or uninstalled during this
  battery.
- Accessibility service **off** for the general and app-launch tests.
- No scrcpy, screen recording or other host-side capture during the timed runs.
- CPU units: the system-wide idle figure is the share of the **whole 4-core SoC**; the
  per-process table is the share of **one core**. This is the unit printed by
  `tcl-bench.sh`/`tcl-launcher-bench.sh`; older docs incorrectly said "of one core".
- App "first frame" is `am start -W` **WaitTime**. `TotalTime` is not used: in the
  Settings→Home sequence Android joins the two launches and `TotalTime` reports ~3.7 s that
  did not happen (the same correction recorded in the v0.3.3 report).

Three independent `tcl-launcher-bench.sh` runs of the same installed 0.5.2 build, pooled by raw
sample rather than by averaging medians:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12:25 | 36 | 13 / 16 / 17 / 17 ms | 631 / 642 / 645 ms | 0.4% | 414 / 483 ms | 39 / 181 ms | 14.9% | 23 MB |
| 13:26 | 36 | 13 / 17 / 19 / 19 ms | 631 / 641 / 642 ms | 0.1% | 488 / 539 ms | 36 / 164 ms | 17.3% | 18 MB |
| 13:36 | 60 | 13 / 15 / 16 / 16 ms | 632 / 641 / 644 ms | 0.3% | 525 / 962 ms | 36 / 152 ms | 15.6% | 18 MB |
| **Pooled** | **132** | **13 / 16 / 17 / 19 ms** | **632 / 641 / 645 ms** | **0.3%** | **466 / 962 ms** | **38 / 164 ms** | **15.6%** | **18 MB** |

- **Key response** is one vsync in practice: 13 ms typical, 17 ms p99, 19 ms worst case over 132
  keys. **Full movement** is 632 ms because the normal mode animates the dock; this is a UI
  choice, not input lag (see ultralight below).
- **Cold start:** 14 of 15 runs were 402–540 ms (p50 466 ms); one run took 962 ms. That outlier
  was the first start after a long idle/60-key sequence, not a sustained regression.
- **Home return:** p50 38 ms, p95 164 ms, max 181 ms over 35 returns.
- **Idle:** the launcher itself draws **0.0–0.1 fps** and uses **0% of one core**. The 14.9–17.3%
  system-wide figure is the rest of Android; the biggest consumers are the always-on Google
  hotword (`voice_ap` 26.6% + `katniss:interactor` 26.2% of one core) and TCL's `sitatvservice`
  (6.2% of one core). See "Idle CPU" below.
- **PSS:** 18 MB at the end of the run, 16 MB after the 10–15 Home returns; the earlier 12:25 run
  measured 23/21 MB, so the observed range is 16–23 MB.

### Stock reference: the animation state matters

The stock Home has two very different states, and they must not be mixed:

| Stock run | Idle CPU | Idle fps | Nav frames / jank | MemAvailable | Swap used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00:26 (3 reps, steady state) | 57.3% | 60.0 | 206 / 85.9% | 427 MB | 391 MB |
| 13:49 (5 reps, fresh/cold) | 20.6% | **3.7** | 336 / 86.6% | 380 MB | 454 MB |
| **Eddy v0.5.2** (13:07, 5 reps) | **14.5%** | **0.0** | 275 / 2.18% | **525 MB** | **341 MB** |

The 13:49 sample was taken 45 s after enabling the stock launcher; its feed animation was not
running at 60 fps yet (3.7 fps), so its 20.6% CPU is **not** a fair steady-state baseline. The
00:26 run is the stock Home as a user sees it after it settles: 60 fps, 57.3% CPU, 85.9% jank. The
13:49 navigation sample still shows 86.6% jank, so the stock Home remains the same slow animated
Home when interacted with. The robust head-to-head is the navigation jank (86.6% vs 2.18%) and the
memory/app-start tables, not the cold 20.6% CPU sample.

| Mode | Key response p50 / p95 / max | Full movement p50 / p95 / max | Janky frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal (`light=false`) | 13 / 16 / 19 ms (132 keys) | 632 / 641 / 645 ms | 0.3% (launcher runs) |
| Ultralight (`light=true`) | 16 / 32 / 32 ms (20 keys) | **16 / 32 / 45 ms** | **0.0% of 23** |

The focus jump falls from ~632 ms to ~16 ms (about 39× faster). Key response is not lower in

same behavior. The 20-key probe ran while a parallel run sampled CPU over ADB (no key input); the
p50 is the robust figure, and the p95/tail may include a few ms of noise.

The on-TV options menu is: *Seu nome*, *Mensagem*, *Cor de destaque*, *Movimento suave* (the

## Idle CPU: where the 14.5–17.3% goes

The launcher contributes 0% of one core and 0 fps. The dominant fixed cost is the Google
hotword: `voice_ap` + `katniss:interactor` ≈ 52.8% of one core ≈ 13.2% of the whole 4-core SoC.
TCL's `sitatvservice` adds ~6.2% of one core. Background streaming apps that are still resident
add a few tenths. The hotword was **not** disabled: it is a functional feature, and the task
rules forbid disabling more system components without approval. Turning off "Ok Google"
hands-free in the Assistant settings is the first recommended next optimization; it should cut
idle CPU by roughly 13 percentage points of the 4-core capacity.

## What changed in 0.5.2 and what did not

- The installed APK is 0.5.2 (versionCode 13), not 0.5.0/0.5.1. The public source now matches it.
- The Globoplay-button accessibility remap remains off during the general battery; it redirects
  any foreground Globoplay event and would hijack the `am start` tests. Its key-delivery path was
  exercised separately: with the service **off**, 100 real D-pad events across two clean runs
  took p50 3 / p95 4 / p99 5 / max 6 ms from the input device to the Home. The service-on runs
  were both invalidated by collisions (a reboot and an app timeline), so its overhead is **not
  measured**; the service requests no key filtering by design (`key_remap.xml`), so it is not
  expected to add input delay.
- No firmware, bootloader, package list, app data or account was changed.
- The stock launcher remains installed but disabled; the recovery path is documented in
  `docs/RECOVERY.md`.

## Idle with the apps closed — a controlled head-to-head

The idle figures above were taken while the streaming apps were still resident, so the system-wide
total carried their leftovers. `tcl-idle-clean.sh` closes Netflix, YouTube, Globoplay, Spotify and
Settings first, then samples for 120 s. The stock launcher was enabled and set as Home for the
stock sample and restored afterwards (verified in the log: Home back to
`br.eddy.tvhome`, `com.google.android.tvlauncher` back to `enabled=3`).

| Sample | Focus | CPU, % of the 4-core SoC | Launcher, % of one core | Idle fps | Frames in 120 s | MemAvailable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stock 14:06 | `com.google.android.tvlauncher` | 19.4 | **10.5** | 3.80 | 456 | 596 MB |
| stock 14:12 | `com.google.android.tvlauncher` | 19.3 | **10.5** | 3.79 | 455 | 620 MB |
| Eddy 14:03 | `br.eddy.tvhome` | 26.8 | **0.9** | 0.17 | 20 | 681 MB |
| Eddy 14:10 | `br.eddy.tvhome` | 14.0 | **0.0** | 0.03 | 4 | 717 MB |

What this settles:

- **The launcher's own cost is 10.5% of one core on the stock Home and 0.0–0.9% on Eddy TV**, and
  it redraws at 3.8 fps against 0.03–0.17 fps. That is the number that belongs to the launcher.
- **The system-wide total is noisy and it is not the launcher's.** The first Eddy sample came out
  at 26.8% and the second at 14.0%: the difference is the Google hotword, which was still hot
  from the assistant test in the first window (`voice_ap` 23.3% + `katniss:interactor` 23.3% of
  one core against 27.0% + 26.8% in the stock windows). The stock pair, measured back to back,
  repeats to within 0.1 pp, so the stock Home is steady and the total follows the voice stack.
- **Memory moves the right way**: 596–620 MB available on the stock Home against 681–717 MB on
  Eddy TV, with the swap used equal in both (≈ 293 MB, the zRAM floor).

Raw values: `benchmarks/results/data/2026-10-19-v0.5.2/idle-clean-pair.csv`.

## Behaviour found while running the battery

Three things that are not performance numbers but change how the TV must be driven, and that
anyone automating this TV will hit:

1. **`KEYCODE_WAKEUP` is translated into a HOME intent on this TV.** Sending it while Netflix was
   in the foreground produced
   `ActivityManager: START u0 {act=android.intent.action.MAIN cat=[android.intent.category.HOME]
   cmp=br.eddy.tvhome/...}` 1.2 s later, and the launcher took the screen back. A wake key is
   therefore *not* a harmless no-op here: `scripts/tcl-app-start-test.sh` wakes the screen once
   before the timer starts and never again during the measurement, because a capture in the middle
   of the run would push the app under test out of the way.
2. **The Netflix window cannot be captured.** With Netflix in the foreground and the screen on
   (`mWakefulness=Awake`, `Display Power: state=ON`), `screencap` returns **0 bytes** and
   `uiautomator dump` exposes only the containers (`gibbonMain`, `gibbon`, `playerContainer`) with
   no title rows, because the catalogue is drawn in a custom surface behind `FLAG_SECURE`.
   `dumpsys gfxinfo com.netflix.ninja` counts 2 frames for the whole run for the same reason.
   The automated detectors `tcl-app-ready.sh` and `tcl-app-frames.sh` were written for this and
   both come back empty on Netflix; `tcl-app-assisted.sh` measures it with a person instead.
   **This is the DRM working as intended, not a fault** — and it is also why the catalogue time
   cannot be certified over ADB.
3. **Ultralight mode does not hide the greeting or the weather line.** The code comment on
   `MainActivity.light` says "apps only, no glow, title, weather or springs", but
   `updateGreeting()` sets the brand, greeting, subtitle and weather text the same way in both
   modes; only the *motion* changes (no springs, no glow, no tile title, no entry animation, and
   the focus jumps instead of gliding: 1 frame drawn per move against 38 in normal mode, measured
   by `tcl-launcher-features.sh`). The weather request is also not gated by the mode: it is cached
   and refreshed at most once an hour. Earlier wording in this repository that said ultralight
   "never fetches the weather" was wrong and has been corrected.

Also confirmed on this TV, by the system's own state rather than by eye:

- **HDMI number keys 1-3 open the TV app on the matching input** (`com.tcl.tv` takes focus for
  each). Key 4 does nothing because that port has no device connected — the launcher indexes the
  HDMI-type inputs it finds, and there are three of them here. Two HDMI ports report
  `state: 0` (connected) and two `state: 1`.
- **DRM is present and running**: `android.hardware.drm@1.1-service.widevine`, plus clearkey,
  `drmserver` and `mediadrmserver`.
- **Chromecast and Google services are installed**: `com.google.android.apps.mediashell`,
  `com.google.android.gms`, `com.google.android.katniss`, `com.tcl.micmanager`. Whether casting
  and far-field voice actually work still needs a person.

## Smoke test: the voice check is intermittent on this TV

The 24-check smoke test failed the three voice checks in one of three runs on 2026-10-19, even
though the assistant was working. Two separate causes, both now handled in
`scripts/tcl-smoke.sh`:

- **The Netflix step holds the focus.** Netflix draws in a DRM-protected surface and keeps the
  focus longer than a normal app, so invoking the assistant over it leaves the microphone
  closed. The script now waits until the launcher is actually focused before invoking the voice.
- **The first invocation can be dropped.** With the launcher in focus, the assistant still did
  not open the microphone on the first attempt in one run out of three, and worked perfectly when
  the same command was repeated immediately after. The check now tries up to three times and only
  fails if all three fail; the log of the last attempt is kept in `private/` for diagnosis.

With both fixes, three consecutive runs were **24/24 OK** at 15:45, 15:47 and 15:48. The
intermittency is a property of the TV's voice stack, not of the launcher: the launcher does not
sit in the voice path at all.

## Pending physical checks

These cannot be certified over ADB and must be done with the TV/remote in hand:

1. Unplug the TV from power and time the full boot with an external stopwatch (bootloader/kernel
   time is invisible to Android). The `adb reboot` numbers only cover the Android part.
2. Press the remote's microphone button and confirm the microphone actually captures voice (the
   smoke test proves the HAL opens and the assistant reaches the server, not the audio quality).
3. Confirm when the Netflix catalogue is truly ready/usable, and whether a profile must be
   selected first.
4. HDMI 2 and 3 with real sources (only one HDMI input was connected), plus Live TV/AV if present.
5. Listen to the speakers/soundbar and confirm DRM-protected playback (Widevine process is up;
   actual playback is visual/audio).
6. Chromecast from a phone and control through Google Home (MediaShell process is up, casting not
   exercised).
7. Wi-Fi-only operation (ADB is over Ethernet; switching would drop the test link).
8. Screensaver after the configured idle time.
9. 8-hour idle stability and weeks of daily use of the debloat list.

## Validity notes (runs that were discarded)

- The accessibility key-path test was attempted three times. The service-off path is clean: 14:15
  (60 events, p50 2 / p95 4 / p99 4 / max 4 ms) and 14:42 (40 events, p50 3 / p95 4 / p99 6 /
  max 6 ms), pooled to 100 events at p50 3 / p95 4 / p99 5 / max 6 ms. Both service-on runs are
  discarded: 14:18 overlapped a reboot, and 14:43 overlapped a parallel Netflix timeline run
  (started 14:43:22) and logged 0/40 keys. Accessibility-on key latency on v0.5.2 is therefore
  **not measured**; only the service-off path is published.
- The ultralight 20-key probe at 14:03 overlapped a CPU-only idle sample from a parallel run; the
  p50 movement (16 ms) is robust, the p95/tail may include a few milliseconds of noise.
- The stock battery at 13:49 is a cold sample (feed at 3.7 fps); its 20.6% CPU is not the
  steady-state baseline. The 00:26 stock run (60 fps) is used for the steady-state CPU comparison.
